    The county's own slice

    Curry County government itself keeps only its permanent rate of $0.5996 per $1,000 of assessed value — roughly 7¢ of every property tax dollar collected in the county. Everything else on a tax bill (schools, fire districts, cities, ports, libraries, health districts, urban renewal) is collected by the County Assessor but spent by ~48 separate independent taxing districts, not by county government.

    This view shows only what the county government itself budgets and spends, from the FY2025–26 proposed budget.

    "Non-Departmental" is excluded from the chart at right: it is mostly a pass-through fund (property tax, timber revenue, PILT payments) that gets transferred out to fund the departments shown, so including both would double count. The Road Capital Improvement Fund is also excluded: it's mostly a multi-year reserve for future road projects, not one year's operating spend, and would swamp every other department if included at face value.

    About this app

    This is an unofficial, independently built estimator. It is meant to make Curry County, Oregon's property tax system easier to understand — it is not an official tax calculator and should not be used to predict an exact bill. Actual bills depend on your parcel's exact code area, any exemptions, compression (Oregon's Measure 5 rate limits), special assessments, and mid-year changes. For your exact bill, contact the Curry County Assessor's Office.

    Data vintage

    All figures are for fiscal year 2025–2026. Tax rates and county budgets are set annually — this data will be out of date starting July 2026 and should be refreshed for FY2026–27.

    Sources

      How composite rates were computed

      The source "Tax Rates by Code Area" document is a dense, multi-column, OCR-scanned PDF. Rather than trust its printed per-code-area subtotal at face value, this app recomputes each code area's composite rate by summing the individual district rates that apply to it, using the certified per-district rates from the Tax Roll Summary. The printed subtotal is kept only as a cross-check.

      In of the ~45 code areas, the recomputed sum disagrees with the source PDF's printed subtotal by more than a rounding error — including three of the six "representative" areas used for the simple picker (Gold Beach, Pistol River, and Brookings-Harbor). Some of this looks explainable (e.g. the printed total appears to exclude urban renewal division-of-tax rates, which are not an additional line item on a bill), and some looks like OCR/transcription error in the source table. This app uses the recomputed sum as the more reliable figure, and flags every affected area below so you can judge for yourself.

      Urban renewal note

      Urban renewal districts (Brookings Urban Renewal, Gold Beach Urban Renewal) work by reallocating a portion of the tax already collected within their district boundary, rather than adding a new rate on top of a bill. The rates shown for these are the source document's reference/summary rates and are included in the composite calculation for completeness, but treat them as informational.

      Limitations

      • Estimates only — not an official calculator.
      • Does not account for exemptions, compression, or special assessments.
      • "Representative" code areas are the most common configuration for a named area, not a guarantee for any specific parcel.
      • County budget figures are from the proposed FY2025–26 budget and may shift slightly at the Approved/Adopted stage.
